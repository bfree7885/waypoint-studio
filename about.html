About

Capture what you find. Learn why it matters.

Waypoint Studio helps people observe, discover, and understand the outdoors — privacy-first tools for attention in the field, not a feed to scroll.

Mission

  • Observe.
  • Discover.
  • Understand.

We help curious people pay attention to landscapes, weather, light, wildlife sign, and place — without becoming a social network, a school, or a content mill.

We are a trusted guide, not a teacher assigning work. You decide what happens next.

The Waypoint ecosystem

Four active public efforts, each with a clear job:

  • Dashboard — Waypoint Studio’s core product. What is happening around you, what is changing, and what is worth your attention.
  • Shed Hunting — a sibling destination (ShedHunting.org, powered by Waypoint). The overview answers whether today favors searching; the map is where to look. Likelihood and habitat interest, never a claim that an antler is present.
  • Waypoint Deck — a distinct local-first Linux field-computing project, not a Studio web app.
  • Waypoint Publishing — editorial stories and interpretation, including Deep Forest Dispatch.

Dashboard is the Studio software. Shed Hunting sits beside it. Deck is a separate field computer. Publishing is reading, not another application. Photography tools under Scenes remain available at existing URLs but are not a current public Studio product.

Shared philosophy

Every Waypoint experience inherits the same quiet commitments:

  • Curiosity — the job is careful attention, not content consumption.
  • Privacy-first — field notes, finds, and personal records stay on your device by default. When a network path is required, we say so.
  • Education and context — tools teach judgment; they do not pretend unfinished features are ready.
  • Useful technology — capabilities earn their place by helping you see, or understand, the world outdoors.
  • Calm interfaces — readable and unhurried; motion supports understanding, not addiction.

We do not build a follower economy, engagement manipulation, or meaningless gamification. No streaks that punish absence. No public popularity rankings inside field tools.

What we refuse

  • Selling user data to advertisers
  • Deceptive or disguised advertising
  • Sponsored or affiliate content that is not clearly identifiable
  • Letting advertisers or sponsors control scientific, educational, or editorial conclusions
  • Intrusive formats that make the product unpleasant — disruptive pop-ups, forced autoplay, and similar patterns
  • Engagement algorithms and dopamine loops
  • Public popularity rankings inside field tools
  • Follower economies and gamified pressure
  • Manipulative “streaks,” school-style pressure theater, or dark patterns
  • Pretending unfinished features are available
