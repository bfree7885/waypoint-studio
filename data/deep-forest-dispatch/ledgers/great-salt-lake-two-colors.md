## Claim plan (supported)

| Claim | Support |
| --- | --- |
| Great Salt Lake is a terminal lake in northern Utah; remnant of Lake Bonneville | NASA EO (ISS015-E-5815 caption); USGS Utah Water Science Center |
| Railroad rock-fill causeway (completed 1959) divides north (Gunnison Bay) and south (Gilbert Bay) | Utah Geological Survey “A Lake Divided”; USGS; PLOS ONE White et al. 2015 |
| Restricted exchange through limited openings / porous fill | UGS; USGS Deep Brine Layer pages; PLOS ONE |
| ~Most freshwater inflows enter the south arm (Bear, Weber, Jordan) | UGS; PLOS ONE (~95% streamflow to south) |
| North arm typically much higher salinity; often near halite saturation | UGS north-arm salinity notes; PLOS ONE averages since 1966 |
| Color contrast linked to different microbial communities (e.g. *Dunaliella salina* / *Halobacterium* north; greener algae e.g. *Dunaliella viridis* south) | NASA EO algae caption; peer-reviewed phytoplankton context |
| Colors and contrast vary with lake level, season, openings/bridges, and biology — not a permanent paint job | UGS bridge/opening notes; USGS multi-decadal change framing; NASA EO drought/level context |
| Astronaut/satellite imagery shows the east–west color divide along the causeway | NASA EO oblique ISS043-E-123891; Skylab/ISS records |

## Caveats (must not overclaim)

- Do not say “one side has bacteria” as the whole explanation — physical restriction + salinity first.
- Do not treat north/south salinities as fixed numbers forever; cite ranges/averages carefully.
- Causeway openings, culvert closures, and the late-2016 bridge altered exchange; mention without detailed engineering claims beyond sources.
- Shoreline salt crusts and shallow depth also affect appearance locally.
