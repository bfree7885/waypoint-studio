## Claim plan (supported)

| Claim | Support |
| --- | --- |
| Lenticular clouds form in mountain/lee waves when stable air is forced over terrain | Met Office lee waves; NWS Albuquerque ACSL feature |
| Rising air cools; condensation near wave crests; descending air warms and evaporates cloud | Met Office; NASA EO lenticular explainers |
| Cloud appears nearly stationary while air flows through — continuous condensation/evaporation | NWS ABQ ACSL; Met Office; NASA EO (“constantly building… dissipating”) |
| Stacked “plates” can form with alternating moist/dry layers | Standard lee-wave / lenticular descriptions (Met Office; textbooks mirrored by NWS) |
| Common downwind of major ranges (e.g. Rockies, Andes, NZ Southern Alps); often winter/spring when winds aloft are strong | NWS ABQ; NASA EO NZ Taieri Pet; NASA EO Antarctic examples |
| Presence can indicate mountain-wave turbulence relevant to aviation (non-alarmist) | NWS ABQ; Met Office rotors note |

## Caveats

- Avoid “the cloud isn’t moving” — clarify standing *pattern* vs frozen air.
- Waves can exist without visible cloud if air is too dry.
- Do not invent UFO sensationalism; mention resemblance only to set the hook, then explain.
- Do not invent Waypoint detection products.
