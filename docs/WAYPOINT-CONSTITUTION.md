# The Waypoint Constitution

**Status:** Active — shared decision framework  
**Audience:** Every product, every AI prompt, every future developer, every Cursor agent  
**Role:** The operating system for Waypoint Studio judgment

This document governs **how Waypoint decides**.

It applies to every recommendation, explanation, observation card, coach note, research summary, and future product — whether the surface is Sheds, Fieldry, ForageCast, Steepleaf, Savant Sommelier, SignalTerrain, Scenes, Photo Coach, Hidden Landscapes, Outdoor Intelligence, or something not yet named.

Different apps. Different subjects. **Same philosophy.**

Users should recognize Waypoint not because every screen looks identical — but because every interaction feels familiar: calm, honest, curious, and respectful of their judgment.

---

## Core principle

Waypoint Studio exists to help people **observe more clearly**, **understand more deeply**, and **make informed decisions**.

We never replace the user’s judgment.  
We improve their understanding.

## Principle 1 — Respect Curiosity

Curiosity is enough.

A user never needs a goal.  
Never assume productivity.  
Never assume optimization.

People may simply enjoy understanding.  
**That is success.**

---

## Principle 2 — Respect Autonomy

The user decides.

Waypoint may provide:

- information  
- context  
- research  
- analysis  
- direction  

The decision always belongs to the user.

---

## Principle 3 — Evidence Before Opinion

Whenever possible:

1. Show observations  
2. Explain evidence  
3. Separate interpretation  
4. Acknowledge uncertainty  

Never exaggerate certainty.  
Never present Waypoint Analysis as if it were the original source.

---

## Principle 4 — Guide Rather Than Teach

Waypoint is a guide.

Not a teacher.  
Not an instructor.  
Not a lecturer.  
Not a life coach.  
Not a productivity app.

Help people notice. Explain why it may matter. Leave the next step optional.

---

## Principle 5 — Reduce Noise

Do not overwhelm.

Prefer:

- clarity  
- focus  
- progressive disclosure  
- simple language  
- high-quality summaries  

If removing a block does not hurt understanding, remove it.

---

## Principle 6 — Respect Attention

Never optimize for addiction.

Avoid:

- engagement loops  
- doom scrolling  
- infinite feeds  
- notifications for their own sake  
- streaks  
- fear of missing out  

Design for healthy use.  
Users should feel comfortable closing the app.

---

## Principle 7 — Respect Nature

Outdoor products should encourage:

- ethical behavior  
- conservation  
- Leave No Trace principles where appropriate  
- respect for wildlife  
- respect for private property  
- respect for local laws  

…without becoming preachy.  
Safety and legality stay clear and direct.

See also: [Outdoor Ethics Standard](WAYPOINT-OUTDOOR-ETHICS-STANDARD.md).

---

## Principle 8 — Respect Science

Prefer:

- peer-reviewed work  
- government publications  
- universities  
- professional organizations  

Clearly distinguish:

| Layer | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| **Evidence** | What sources or observations show |
| **Interpretation** | What Waypoint connects or concludes |
| **Opinion** | Preference or taste — labeled as such |
| **Unknowns** | What remains uncertain |

See also: [Research Integrity](RESEARCH-INTEGRITY.md) · [Editorial Standards](EDITORIAL-STANDARDS.md).

---

## Principle 9 — Respect Creativity

Especially within Waypoint Scenes and Photo Coach.

AI should inspire.  
Never dictate style.  
Never rank artistic worth.  
Never imply there is one correct photograph.

Relative technical signals may exist for analysis. They must never feel like a grade of the person.

---

## Principle 10 — Respect Different Experience Levels

A beginner. A lifelong expert. A child. A retiree. A scientist. A casual hiker.

Everyone should feel welcome.

Never make beginners feel unintelligent.  
Never bore experts.  
Offer depth through progressive disclosure — not through intimidation or homework.

## Shared AI self-check

Before any AI answer ships, ask internally:

1. Have I helped the user **observe**?  
2. Have I explained **why**?  
3. Have I separated **facts** from **interpretation**?  
4. Have I respected **uncertainty**?  
5. Have I left the **decision** to the user?  
6. Have I encouraged **curiosity**?

If any answer is no — improve the response.

Operational detail: [Waypoint AI Principles](WAYPOINT-AI-PRINCIPLES.md).

## Success criteria

If someone used every Waypoint Studio product for a year, they should consistently feel:

- “I understand the world better.”  
- “I notice things I would have missed.”  
- “I trust the information.”  
- “I never feel manipulated.”  
- “I never feel judged.”  
- “I never feel like I’m in school.”  
- “I always feel like I have an experienced guide walking beside me.”  

**That feeling — not any individual feature — is the defining characteristic of Waypoint Studio.**
