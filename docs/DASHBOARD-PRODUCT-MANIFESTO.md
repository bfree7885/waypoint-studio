# Dashboard Product Manifesto

**Waypoint Outdoor Dashboard**  
**Status:** Permanent product specification  
**Authority:** When Dashboard product arguments conflict, this document wins.  
**Companion philosophy:** Waypoint Studio Product Standards apply to every surface; this manifesto defines Dashboard alone.

---

## Preamble

People do not need another place that shows the weather.

They need a place that tells them the truth about the day outside—near them, right now—and what that truth asks of them.

Dashboard exists because the outdoors is not a forecast. It is a living field: light that changes by the hour, air you can feel or cannot trust, water that rises or falls, sky that opens or closes, alerts that matter and noise that does not. Most apps hand you instruments. Dashboard hands you orientation.

This is not a feature list. It is a covenant.

If we forget why someone opens this every morning, we will slowly rebuild a weather website with prettier language. That is the failure mode we refuse.

---

## The one job

Dashboard has one job.

Answer this question, every day, for the place that is truly theirs:

> **What is happening outside near me today, what matters most, and what should I do about it?**

Everything on the first screen must serve that sentence.  
Everything that does not serve it waits.  
Everything that fights it is cut.

Dashboard is not many products wearing one name. It is one briefing with depth available when the day demands it.

---

## Who it is for

**Dashboard is for people who go outside**—or intend to—and want to begin the day oriented: walkers, runners, photographers, anglers, parents planning a park hour, travelers checking the trailhead before they pack, anyone who cares what the sky, air, light, and water are doing *here*.

They may be expert or beginner. They do not need to become meteorologists to deserve clarity.

**Dashboard is not for:**

- People shopping for a customizable instrument panel
- People who want national spectacle, storm porn, or map theater as the product
- People who want social proof, streaks, or “engagement”
- People who want us to invent a hometown so the screen looks full
- Operators monitoring systems—this is a field briefing, not a status console

If the primary user feels like a spectator of weather media, we have the wrong product.

## 1. Why will someone open Dashboard every morning?

They open it because the day outside is not the day inside their head.

Overnight, the world shifted: a front moved, the river rose, the air thickened, the light went soft, a warning appeared, the window for a walk narrowed. Other apps will show pieces of that shift. Dashboard exists to **settle the morning**—to turn uncertainty into a calm, honest decision.

They open it for three reasons that do not expire:

1. **Orientation** — “What is it like out there, near me, today?”  
2. **Priority** — “Of everything true, what matters most before I leave?”  
3. **Action** — “What should I do—go, wait, change the plan, watch for this?”

The habit is not dopamine. The habit is trust.

A morning open should feel like checking with a quiet, competent friend who knows the place and will not exaggerate: *Here’s the day. Here’s what counts. Here’s what I’d do.*

If they open Dashboard and leave more anxious, more scattered, or more entertained than oriented, the morning product failed—even if every number was correct.

---

## 2. What emotional experience should the first 10 seconds create?

**Calm certainty without false certainty.**

In the first ten seconds, the person should feel:

- **Located** — this is about *my* outdoors, not a generic nation or a guessed city  
- **Briefed** — the day has a character; I can feel it before I study it  
- **Prioritized** — one or two things rise above the rest; I am not drowning in equal facts  
- **Capable** — I know what to do next, or what to watch, without being scolded  
- **Respected** — the product did not invent, hype, or hide uncertainty

The emotional arc is short and deliberate:

1. **Recognition** — “This is my place / my morning.”  
2. **Atmosphere** — “I understand the day’s feel.”  
3. **Weight** — “I see what matters.”  
4. **Agency** — “I know my move.”

Not urgency. Not alarmism. Not cheerful emptiness. Not “wow, look at all we know.”

If the first ten seconds feel like opening a control room, we lost.  
If they feel like receiving a field briefing at dawn, we won.

---

## 3. What makes Dashboard fundamentally different?

Dashboard is an **Outdoor Operating System**: a daily operating layer for life outdoors—not a weather site, not a map product, not a gadget companion, not a widget wall.

Competitors excel at instruments, maps, or forecasts. We excel at **decision-grade orientation for the day near you**.

### Apple Weather

Apple Weather is elegant meteorology for a location: temperature, conditions, hourly and daily arcs, polished and trustworthy as a forecast surface.

**Dashboard differs:** We do not stop at “what the weather is.” We answer what the outdoors *asks*—across sky, air, light, water, and hazard—then name what matters most and what to do. Apple shows the climate of the hour. We brief the day as a field of action.

### Windy

Windy is a cathedral of atmospheric visualization: models, layers, motion, power-user depth.

**Dashboard differs:** We are not a map theater. We refuse spectacle as the first experience. Depth exists when the day needs it; the first screen is a human briefing, not a playground of layers. Power without orientation is not our product.

### Garmin

Garmin is performance and device intelligence for athletes and outdoor athletes: metrics, training, body, route, achievement.

**Dashboard differs:** We are not a coach of pace or a ledger of effort. We orient the *environment* so the person can choose wisely—whether they train, wander, photograph, or simply step outside with a child. The outdoors is the subject, not the user’s body score.

### Gaia GPS

Gaia GPS is a map and navigation companion: terrain, tracks, offline confidence for moving through land.

**Dashboard differs:** We are not primarily “how do I get there.” We are “what is it like *here today*, and what should I do before or as I go.” Navigation products own the path. We own the morning judgment that decides whether the path makes sense.

### Google Weather

Google Weather is convenient, glanceable, and fused into search and phone habits: quick answers, ads of attention, broad utility.

**Dashboard differs:** We are not a glance snippet or a portal. We are a dedicated outdoor briefing that stays local-first, privacy-respecting, and free of engagement logic. Convenience without depth of place is not enough; depth without calm clarity is not enough either. We hold both: near-me truth, then action.

### The sharp line

| They tend to be… | Dashboard is… |
|------------------|---------------|
| Forecast, map, or metric | Briefing |
| Many equal facts | Ranked meaning |
| Instruments first | Judgment first |
| Spectacle or utility glance | Morning orientation |
| Your body or your route | Your outdoors today |

If a competitor already does something better as an instrument, we do not out-instrument them on the first screen. We out-*orient* them.

## 4. If the user only looks at ONE screen, what should they know?

Before they go outside, one screen must leave them knowing:

1. **Where this briefing applies** — a real place they own or chose; never a fabricated hometown  
2. **What the outdoors feels like today** — the character of the day in human language, grounded in truth  
3. **What matters most** — the one or two forces that should shape the plan (air, storm, heat, flood, light, freeze, wind—whatever is actually decisive *today*)  
4. **What to do** — a clear posture: go, go with care, wait, choose a different window, or watch a specific condition  
5. **Whether the briefing is fresh and complete enough to trust** — honesty about live, cached, partial, or offline—without making trust chrome the hero

That is the whole contract of the single screen.

They should not need a second tap to know if today is ordinary, beautiful, or consequential.  
They should not need to assemble meaning from a grid of equal tiles.  
They should leave able to pack, time, or postpone with dignity.

**The one-screen test:**  
If they closed the product after that screen and walked to the door, would they be safely and wisely oriented? If not, the screen failed—regardless of how much else we know.

---

## 5. What should never appear on the first screen?

The first screen is sacred. It is not a warehouse.

**Never on the first screen:**

- A wall of equal widgets, gauges, or cards competing for attention  
- Product chrome that announces versions, recovery modes, or internal architecture  
- Customize / rearrange / “make it yours” as a primary invitation  
- Provider status, pipeline health, or technical monitoring language as the main story  
- National or default place presented as if it were *their* place  
- Invented conditions, filled-in emptiness, or confident fiction when data is missing  
- Engagement bait: streaks, scores-as-grades, social proof, artificial urgency, scarcity  
- Secondary domains presented as peers when they are not decisive today  
- Cluttered alert lists that treat every notice as equal drama  
- Homework energy: quizzes, lessons owed, progress through a curriculum  
- Map spectacle, model playgrounds, or dense legend chrome as the opening act  
- Anything that requires the user to *assemble* the answer we already owe them

If it helps power users later, it can live deeper.  
If it helps no one decide whether to go outside, it does not belong at all.

---

## 6. What should the first screen feel like?

**One composition. One briefing. One outdoor morning.**

It should feel like:

- Standing at the threshold with someone who has already looked at the sky  
- A quiet desk at dawn, not a trading floor  
- Natural light and weather character—not abstract decoration pretending to be nature  
- Language a thoughtful ranger might use: plain, precise, kind, never theatrical  
- Hierarchy you can feel: atmosphere → what matters → what to do  
- Space to breathe; motion only where it clarifies presence, not where it performs

It should **not** feel like:

- A dashboard of parts  
- A news ticker of outdoor anxiety  
- A science fair of every sensor we could wire  
- A brand-empty utility that could belong to anyone after the logo is removed  
- Dark urgency cosplay or cheerful emptiness

**Feel words we protect:** calm, clear, local, honest, decisive, welcoming, curious.  
**Feel words we refuse on first open:** busy, clever, loud, customizable-first, speculative, national-generic, frozen, fake-complete.

The first screen is a place. Treat it like one.

## 7. Dashboard’s core principles

These principles are binding. They settle arguments.

1. **One job** — Answer the north-star question. Nothing else is the product.  
2. **Briefing over instruments** — Orientation first; depth on demand.  
3. **Near me, truly** — Local-first; place must be real, chosen, or clearly provisional—never faked.  
4. **What matters most** — Rank meaning. Do not present every truth as equal.  
5. **Action is owed** — End in what to do, watch, or wait for—not only what is.  
6. **Trust is visible, not the hero** — Honesty about freshness and gaps; never fabricate to look complete.  
7. **Privacy is default** — Transparent permission; no hidden tracking of place as a growth engine.  
8. **Calm over urgency** — Serious when the day is serious; never addicted to alarm.  
9. **Curiosity without homework** — Invite noticing; never grade the user.  
10. **Outdoor ethics** — Respect wildlife, land, safety, and Leave No Trace in counsel and tone.  
11. **Scientific honesty** — Separate fact, estimate, and interpretation; no pseudoscience.  
12. **One composition** — The first view is a single outdoor briefing, not a collage of products.  
13. **Depth without identity crisis** — Specialty insight may exist; it must not redefine the first screen as a console.  
14. **Simplicity is respect** — Every addition must make the morning clearer or more capable—not denser.

## Emotional design language

**Tone:** Quiet confidence. Field-ready. Adult without being cold. Warm without being cute.

**Voice:** Short sentences when the truth is sharp. Human words for outdoor forces. No jargon theater. No false intimacy. No corporate cheer.

**Calm vs urgency:**

- Default state is calm clarity.  
- Urgency is earned by real hazard or real constraint—and then it is specific, proportional, and actionable.  
- We never manufacture urgency to retain attention.  
- We never soft-pedal danger to keep the mood pretty.

**Relationship to the user:** Coach and companion in the Yellowstone ranger sense—notice, explain, invite—never lecturer, never scold, never scorekeeper.

**Promise in language:** Prefer “here’s what matters” over “look how much we know.” Prefer “we don’t have this yet” over silence filled with fiction.

## Success and failure (product sense)

### A morning is won when

- The person understands the day’s outdoor character near them  
- They know what mattered most before they stepped out  
- They can act—go, adjust, wait—without assembling meaning themselves  
- They trust what they saw, including any limits on freshness or completeness  
- They feel calmer and more capable than before they opened Dashboard  
- Curiosity is intact; fear is proportional; the outdoors still feels inviting when it is safe to be invited

### A morning is lost when

- They leave informed of many facts but unsure what to do  
- The first screen felt like a widget gallery or a monitoring console  
- Place felt fake, national-generic, or dishonestly complete  
- Noise equaled signal; nothing ranked  
- Urgency was theatrical, or danger was blurred  
- They needed a second product to finish the decision we claimed to own  
- They felt managed, graded, or harvested for attention

Correct data with failed orientation is still a failed morning.

## Non-goals (permanent)

Dashboard will not become:

- A weather website that happens to mention outdoors  
- A customizable widget operating system  
- A map-first navigation product  
- A training / metrics / body-performance product  
- A storm-entertainment or model-spectacle product  
- A technical status board for providers and pipelines  
- A social network, streak engine, or engagement farm  
- A quiz, curriculum, or accountability school for nature  
- A product that invents place or conditions to avoid empty honesty  
- A “do everything outdoor” supermarket where every domain is always front-page equal

Depth is allowed. Identity confusion is not.

## Edge days — product promises

These are promises about *behavior of the product in human terms*, not layouts.

### Alert days

When hazard is real, Dashboard becomes clearer, not louder. The briefing elevates what protects life and plan. Action is unmistakable. Secondary beauty does not bury the warning. Calm remains; complacency does not.

### Beautiful days

When the outdoors is generous, say so without padding. Celebrate conditions that invite going out. Do not invent drama to feel useful. A beautiful day still names anything that quietly matters (air, UV, timing of light)—then gets out of the way.

### Conflicted days

When signals disagree—good light but poor air, clear sky but rising water, mild temperature but harsh wind—Dashboard must **name the tension** and **choose a priority**. Conflict is information. The product’s job is judgment under tradeoffs, not a polite list of contradictions.

### No location

Never pretend. Invite a real place with dignity. Explain why near-me matters. Offer only what can be honestly said without a place—and label it as not yet personal. A provisional national or regional shell must never masquerade as “your” hometown.

### Offline / stale / partial

Honesty is the feature. Say what is known, what is old, what is missing. Help the person decide with imperfect information rather than disappearing or fabricating. Partial truth, clearly marked, beats a smooth lie.

## What “Outdoor Operating System” means (in human language)

An Outdoor Operating System is the layer you check so the rest of outdoor life can run well.

Not the boots. Not the map. Not the camera. Not the forecast model.

It is the **morning judgment layer**: the shared sense of what the outdoors is doing near you, what deserves attention, and what posture to take—so walks, photos, trips, fishing, family time, and quiet noticing all start from the same honest ground.

Weather apps tell you the atmosphere.  
Maps tell you the land.  
Devices tell you your effort.

An Outdoor Operating System tells you **how to meet the day outside**.

That is Dashboard.

---

## Closing covenant — what we swear never to become

We swear never to become a prettier weather website.

We swear never to trade orientation for a grid of equal gadgets.

We swear never to invent a hometown, a condition, or a certainty we do not have.

We swear never to scare people for retention, or soothe them past real danger.

We swear never to make the first screen about us—our versions, our providers, our cleverness.

We swear to keep the morning human: one place, one briefing, one clear sense of what matters and what to do.

If we keep this covenant, Dashboard earns the daily open.  
If we break it, no amount of depth will save the product’s soul.

**Observe. Understand. Then go outside—with clear eyes.**
