# TerrainBound Game Bible

Canonical design for the three-region vertical slice and every region that follows.

**TERRAINBOUND IS AN EXPLORATION GAME ABOUT LEARNING TO READ THE EARTH.**

**THERE IS NO ENEMY TO DEFEAT. THE WORLD IS THE MYSTERY.**

---

## Premise

The player is a new field explorer entering a network of field stations that study landscapes, geology, water, weather, the sky, climate, hazards, resources, and environmental change.

The goal is not “complete twelve Earth Science topics.”

The goal is:

**EXPLORE THE WORLD. FIGURE OUT HOW IT WORKS. BECOME CAPABLE OF WORKING FARTHER INTO THE FIELD.**

## Player role

A teenage / young field explorer with basic equipment. Not a soldier, not a superhero, not a child mascot, not a photorealistic adult.

Character arc:

1. **New field explorer** — Cedar Hollow. Guided. Learning how to look.
2. **Capable field scientist** — High Country. Tools earned. Maps that must be proven on the ground.
3. **Independent field investigator** — Sunfall and beyond. The network gives problems, not procedures.

## Ranger Wren

First field mentor. Distinctive silhouette: ranger hat, field vest, radio, patience.

Wren is experienced, calm, observant, slightly dry, and comfortable letting the player think.

Wren is not a classroom teacher, narrator, cheerleader, quest vending machine, or exposition machine.

Preferred voice:

- “Walk it first.”
- “What did you actually see?”
- “One measurement?”
- “Your map says that. Does the ground?”
- “That's possible. What supports it?”
- “You've got enough to make a case.”

Presence across the journey:

| Region | Wren |
| --- | --- |
| Cedar Hollow | Physically present. Relatively involved. |
| High Country | At the station. Speaks less. Lets the land argue. |
| Sunfall | Radio more often than body. The player works the sky. |
| Later regions | Problems, not procedures. |
| High Sierra (design only) | “Looks like you don't need me telling you where to look anymore.” |

## The Field Service

Working spoken name: **the Field Service**.

It is a scientific field network / ranger-research observing service. Not a military, not a secret agency, not a corporation, not a lore encyclopedia.

It exists so stations, equipment, radio, field records, travel, clearance, and observation assignments feel inhabited.

Reveal it environmentally: a painted emblem, a station plaque, a radio call, a notebook stamp. Do not write an org chart.

## Field clearance

Mastery is scientific competence, not XP.

**Field clearance** means the Field Service has enough evidence that the explorer is prepared for the next environment.

The emotional payoff is **the road opens**.

No ranks, stars, badges, medals, report cards, or percentages.

## World structure

Twelve regions. One journey. Teaching order, not topic-number order.

Cedar Hollow → High Country → Sunfall Desert begin the walk. Dark Sky Basin and later regions remain visible on the atlas and closed until earned.

Each region is a **place** with a scientific mystery, not a unit with a worksheet.

## Regional structure

Every playable region should have:

- a field station belonging to the Field Service
- a scientific mystery that *is* the adventure
- optional discoveries (observation ≠ interpretation)
- investigations that require walking the land
- a regional field challenge
- evidence-based mastery that can open the next route
- tools earned by use, never by opening a menu

## Scientific mystery philosophy

Regional stories emerge from questions in the world:

- Why is the water behaving this way?
- What shaped this landscape?
- Where exactly are we?
- Why does the shadow move?
- When should we observe?

No villain, evil corporation, apocalypse, conspiracy, magical threat, monsters, combat, boss, or chosen-one narrative.

## Core loop

**EXPLORE → OBSERVE → INVESTIGATE → GATHER EVIDENCE → REASON → ACT → SEE CONSEQUENCES**

## Mastery philosophy

Evidence, not quizzes. Content completion is not mastery. Opening a tool awards nothing.

Player-facing language: investigation, field note, observation, measurement, evidence, Field Record, field plan, route, observation window, clearance.

Avoid in player UI: lesson, unit, quiz, test, assignment, grade, score, standards codes.

## Failure philosophy

The player cannot die.

No health, lives, injury, GAME OVER, academic FAILED, grades, or percentages.

The principal negative state is **not yet**.

Insufficient evidence: “Not enough evidence yet.”
Weak explanation: the evidence challenges the conclusion.
Incomplete mastery: the next route stays closed.
Future unsafe hazard: field operation is suspended, then explained, then retried.

Loop: **try → observe consequence → reconsider → try again.**

## Character progression

Progression is **capability**.

- Cedar Hollow: Field Tablet / journal, field data
- After Cedar Hollow: trusted with independent field work; High Country opens
- High Country: geospatial tools
- Sunfall: solar observer, celestial clock, orbit model, sky log
- Later: geology, seismic, water, weather, climate, hazard tools — not implemented in this slice

The tablet should feel like *their* accumulated scientific record across regions.

## Field tools

Permanent instruments earned by scientific use. Not loot, not inventory economy.

## Field stations

Shared Field Service identity, adapted to climate:

- Cedar Hollow — wooded, warm, weathered, intimate
- Ridgeline Station — alpine, exposed, rugged, practical
- Sunfall Observatory — desert, solar instruments, dome, night lighting

Shared language: emblem, signage, equipment cases, map board, radio, notebooks, survey markers.

## Field Tablet

Field equipment. Not an LMS, worksheet, or school portal.

Holds journal, discoveries, evidence, datasets, graphs, world map, regional maps, GIS, Field Record, astronomy records, tools.

## Discoveries

Optional. Observation first, interpretation later. Never required for regional mastery.

## Hazards (design rule)

Future hazards arise from Earth-system processes. Preparation and understanding affect outcome. No death. Unsafe decisions may suspend a field operation, then retry.

Major hazards are **not** implemented in the three-region slice.

## Environmental storytelling

The world was studied before the player arrived. Restrained props: notebooks, cases, stakes, instruments, faded markings, radio, personal details. Not every object needs interaction. They should say: people work here.

## Visual direction

Polished stylized indie exploration. Not photoreal. Not educational-browser-prototype.

Three identities:

- Cedar Hollow — green / wet / wooded / intimate
- High Country — cool / open / windy / vertical
- Sunfall — warm / dry / sparse / sky-dominated

Same game. Different places.

**CONTENT-HEAVY UNDERNEATH.**
**LIGHT-FEELING ON THE SURFACE.**

Scientific rigor comes from observation, measurement, comparison, pattern recognition, data interpretation, map interpretation, modeling, prediction, explanation, revision, and transfer — not from excessive prose.

Player reads the environment first. Persistent HUD labels are a last resort.

## Cumulative learning — lock

**REGIONS ARE NOT ISOLATED UNITS.**

Skills learned earlier continue to matter.

- Cedar Hollow scientific reasoning continues everywhere.
- High Country maps / topography / geospatial tools continue across later regions.
- Sunfall seasonal / solar / orbital understanding can recur where scientifically relevant.

## Three content layers — lock

Every future region should contain:

A. REQUIRED CORE INVESTIGATIONS — enough to genuinely demonstrate required understanding.
B. EMBEDDED PRACTICE — short natural reuse of concepts during exploration.
C. OPTIONAL DEPTH — discoveries that do not block progression.

Do not use optional content to compensate for a weak required core.

Learning progression: **NOTICE → MEASURE / COMPARE → USE → TRANSFER.**

A later problem should sometimes require the student to recognize and use prior knowledge without the game reteaching it.

Prefer world evidence, maps, graphs, measurements, models, observations, and decisions over long explanatory panels.

Play rhythm (target, not a timer): 2–5 minutes exploration, then 1–3 minutes focused scientific interaction, then return to movement.

See `docs/CONTENT-DENSITY-AUDIT.md` for the reusable region audit.

## Animation direction

Short, responsive character states: idle, walk, inspect, measure, tablet, talk, observe sky.

Never make students wait through repeated long sequences.

Ambient motion is cheap and regional: water, wind, leaves, dust, clouds. Not a particle storm.

## Audio direction

Optional, muteable, no required files, no music required.

Regional ambience plus quiet interaction ticks (footsteps, tablet, measurement, discovery). Procedural is acceptable.

## UI philosophy

World stays primary. Overlays appear when the player asks. 1366×768 school-laptop target. Calm, readable, diegetic where it helps (Field Observation, Field Tablet).

## Long-term ending (design only)

High Sierra: an integrated Earth-system situation. The game does not tell the player what to measure, where to look, which tool to use, or which Earth system matters.

Overlook. Atlas shows the whole route. Wren, restrained:

“Looks like you don't need me telling you where to look anymore.”

Do not implement this in the three-region slice.

## Non-goals

Teacher dashboard, accounts, multiplayer, AI, combat, death, health, survival meters, crafting, inventory economy, XP, levels, badges, currency, shops, villains, deployment, `apps/terrainbound/`, `terrainbound.org`.

Dark Sky Basin is not playable until a later phase.

## Canvas 2D

The production slice uses Canvas 2D, vanilla JS, no build step. Keep it unless a later review names a specific visual/technical blocker with evidence. Do not silently migrate engines.
